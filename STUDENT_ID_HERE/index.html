Level 1: Classical Bit Foundation

The Semiconductor Electronic Switch

Every classical digital computer is built upon transistors acting as electronic switches. When an input voltage \(V_{in}\) is applied across the base-emitter (or gate-source) junction, it controls the flow of electrons between collector and emitter.

Semiconductor Transfer Condition:
  • \(V_{in} < V_{th}\ (0.7\text{V})\): Cut-Off Region → Transistor is OFF (Open Circuit) → \(I_C \approx 0\) → LED is OFF → Classical Bit 0
  • \(V_{in} \ge V_{th}\ (0.7\text{V})\): Saturation Region → Transistor is ON (Short Circuit) → \(I_C\) maximum → LED is ON → Classical Bit 1
Transistor State: CUT-OFF (Switch Open)
Collector Current \(I_C\): 0.00 mA

Level 2: Inversion Operators

Transistor NOT Gate vs Quantum Pauli-X Gate

In classical electronics, an inverter (NOT gate) is achieved using a common-emitter pull-up transistor circuit. In quantum computing, the bit-flip operator is the Pauli-X Gate.

Classical RTL Inverter

Output taken from Collector \(V_{out}\):

  • \(V_{in} = 0\text{V}\ (0) \implies V_{out} = 5\text{V}\ (1)\)
  • \(V_{in} = 5\text{V}\ (1) \implies V_{out} \approx 0.2\text{V}\ (0)\)

Limitation: Operates strictly on deterministic voltage thresholds.

Quantum Pauli-X Gate

Unitary \(2\times 2\) Hermitian matrix:

\(X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\)
  • \(X|0\rangle = |1\rangle\)
  • \(X|1\rangle = |0\rangle\)
  • \(X(\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle) = \beta|0\rangle + \alpha|1\rangle\)
Domain Input Transformation Operator Output
Classical BJT 0V (Bit 0) RTL Inverter (\(V_{out} = V_{CC} - I_C R_C\)) 5V (Bit 1)
Quantum Pauli-X |0⟩ = [1, 0]T Matrix Multiplication \(X \cdot |\psi\rangle\) |1⟩ = [0, 1]T

Level 3: Physical Bit to Quantum State

From Macroscopic Switch to Probability Amplitudes

A classical transistor stores a bit macroscopically: millions of electrons are either trapped (OFF) or conducting (ON). In contrast, a qubit exists as a linear combination of basis vectors in a complex Hilbert space \(\mathbb{C}^2\):

$$|\psi\rangle = \alpha |0\rangle + \beta |1\rangle, \quad \text{with } |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1$$

Where \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}\) are probability amplitudes. When measured, the wavefunction collapses to \(0\) with probability \(P(0) = |\alpha|^2\) and \(1\) with probability \(P(1) = |\beta|^2\).

The Hadamard Gate (\(H\)): True Quantum Superposition

Applying the Hadamard transform maps deterministic basis states into an equal superposition:

$$H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \implies H|0\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}} \equiv |+\rangle$$ $$H(H|0\rangle) = H|+\rangle = |0\rangle \quad \text{(Wave Interference!)}$$
P(0) = |α|2 100.0%
P(1) = |β|2 0.0%

Level 4: Circuit Architectures

Classical Digital Circuit vs Reversible Quantum Circuit

To directly contrast digital logic with quantum computing, we implement the 1-Bit Half Adder (Sum = \(A \oplus B\), Carry = \(A \cdot B\)) through both architectures.

Classical Transistor Half-Adder

  • XOR gate (8 MOSFETs) for Sum
  • AND gate (6 MOSFETs) for Carry
  • Irreversible: 2 inputs \(\to\) 2 outputs, but bit states merge. Original inputs cannot be recovered.
  • Dissipates Landauer heat: \(Q \ge k_B T \ln 2\).

Quantum Reversible Half-Adder

  • Preserves information via Unitary gates (\(U^\dagger U = I\))
  • CNOT gates compute Sum without erasing inputs
  • Toffoli (CCX) gate computes Carry reversibly
  • Supports Quantum Parallelism: evaluate all 4 input pairs simultaneously in superposition!
Inputs: A = 0, B = 0
Classical Outputs: Sum = 0, Carry = 0
Quantum Register: |A, B, Sum, Carry⟩ = |0000⟩ (100%)

Quantum Error Correction: 3-Qubit Bit-Flip Code

Quantum noise can flip a bit (\(X\)) or flip a phase (\(Z\)). Because the No-Cloning Theorem forbids copying unknown states, we encode \(|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle\) into an entangled logical subspace:

$$|\psi_L\rangle = \alpha |000\rangle + \beta |111\rangle$$

Syndrome ancillas measure parities (\(q_0 \oplus q_1\) and \(q_1 \oplus q_2\)) without measuring \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\), preserving the superposition!

Conceptual Comparison: Classical vs Quantum Noise

“How does classical noise in transistor-based switching compare conceptually with noise affecting quantum states?”

In classical semiconductor systems, information is stored as macroscopic charges or voltages ($V_{out} = V_{ideal} + n(t)$). Transistors have steep, non-linear voltage transfer characteristics that produce noise margins ($V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$). Any thermal noise jitter smaller than these margins is actively restored and suppressed by each succeeding logic gate. Errors only occur if noise causes a macroscopic discrete threshold breach.

In contrast, quantum states exist as delicate linear combinations of complex probability amplitudes in Hilbert space ($|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$). Quantum noise (such as phase damping, thermal relaxation $T_1$, and dephasing $T_2$) continuously perturbs the phase and amplitudes through environmental entanglement. Because of the No-Cloning Theorem, intermediate quantum amplitudes cannot be amplified or copied to restore their signal. To protect quantum information, quantum error correction must detect error syndromes through entangled parity measurements that identify which error occurred without ever measuring the underlying quantum data itself.

Circuit Implementation Tasks & Conceptual Understanding Rubric

Standardized evaluation criteria for university laboratory viva and assessment.

Laboratory Practical Tasks

  1. Task 1 (Physical Semiconductor Switch): Wire a 2N2222 NPN BJT switch on a breadboard. Sweep the base voltage from 0V to 5V and record the collector cutoff-to-saturation threshold using Arduino ADC pin A1.
  2. Task 2 (RTL Inverter vs Pauli-X): Measure the output of an RTL logic inverter. Verify that it maps \(0\text{V} \to 5\text{V}\) and \(5\text{V} \to 0.15\text{V}\). Execute the corresponding Pauli-X quantum circuit in Qiskit and explain why \(X\) can operate on superpositions while the BJT cannot.
  3. Task 3 (Quantum Superposition & Wave Interference): Construct a circuit with a single Hadamard gate on Qiskit Aer (1024 shots) to observe a 50/50 probability distribution. Then add a second consecutive Hadamard gate and verify constructive interference on \(|0\rangle\).
  4. Task 4 (Noise & Repetition Code): Inject a bit-flip error into the 3-qubit repetition circuit. Measure the parity syndrome and demonstrate state recovery without measuring data qubits.
